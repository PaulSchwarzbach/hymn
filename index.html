Radio positioning dataset and studies

HYMN HYbrid Multi-technology Navigation

HYMN is a time-synchronised measurement dataset of UWB, BLE, WiFi FTM, 5G NR and GNSS, recorded against total-station ground truth in an industrial hall whose open gates create an indoor-outdoor transition. This page collects the dataset and the studies built on it.

Dataset

The measurements were recorded on 23 October 2024 in an industrial hall in Torgau, Germany. A driveway crosses the hall, and the gates on each side stayed open throughout the campaign, so the points on the driveway see mixed indoor and outdoor propagation. A measurement plate carrying all receivers was placed on each reference point and held static for three minutes while all systems recorded in parallel. A Leica TS16 total station surveyed every reference point and every infrastructure device at millimetre level.

Reference points. The 48 points form three groups: 36 grid points inside the hall (A02–A13), six points on the driveway inside the hall (row A01), and six points on the driveway outside the gates (T01–T06). The studies below label these groups differently, and each summary names its own zone scheme.

Contents

Processed data
One table per system in CSV, Parquet and pickle format, and a merged index that links the observations of all systems by timestamp and reference point.
Raw logs
The native output of each system, including GNSS observations in RINEX 3 with precise orbit and clock products.
Reference data
Coordinates of the infrastructure and the reference points in a local frame, in UTM33N (EPSG:25833) and in ECEF (EPSG:4978), and the start and end time of every static window.

Scope and limits

  • All recordings are static. The dataset contains no trajectories.
  • GNSS was logged while the plate occupied the 12 driveway points. The 36 hall-grid points have no GNSS observations.
  • The 5G NR data are SNR values and position outputs of the small cell system, not range measurements.
  • Each system logs at its own rate, and the data are not resampled to a common clock.

Quick start

Python 3.11 or 3.12. The example iterates over the merged index and computes WiFi ranging errors against ground truth.

git clone https://github.com/TUD-ITVS/HYMN-dataset
cd HYMN-dataset
python -m pip install -r examples/requirements.txt
python examples/example_iterator.py

IPIN 2026Accepted · talk on 7 October 2026, Rome

From Least Squares to Deep Learning: Benchmarking Indoor Positioning on the HYMN Multi-Technology Dataset

Paul Schwarzbach, Muhammad Ammad

Question. How do range-based methods from least squares to a learned regressor compare on fused UWB, BLE and WiFi ranges, and does the regressor generalise to reference points it has not seen?

Fused UWB, BLE and WiFi FTM ranges at all 48 points. The paper labels the hall grid as indoor and the 12 driveway points as transition.

  • The Bayesian grid filter (BGF) reaches the lowest median error of 0.44 m, but a small fraction of epochs collapse near the grid bounds and stretch its P95 to 6.29 m.
  • A ResNet trained on random epoch-level splits sits close on the median (0.55 m) and has the shortest tail (P95 1.31 m).
  • Holding reference points out of training raises the ResNet's median to 3.22 m. Its random-split advantage reflects interpolation rather than generalisation to unseen locations.

ICRA 2026 Workshop1st Workshop on Robot Meets GNSS and Ranging for Seamless Autonomy · 5 June 2026, Vienna

Bridging the Indoor-Outdoor Gap: Cross-Technology Ranging for Seamless Robot Navigation

Paul Schwarzbach

Question. How do satellite and terrestrial ranging behave outdoors, in the transition zone and indoors, judged by measurement availability and by ranging residuals against ground truth?

Raw ranges of GNSS, UWB, WiFi FTM and BLE. The paper labels the six points outside the gates as outdoor, row A01 as transition and the hall grid as indoor.

  • UWB ranges stay tight inside the hall, with a P95 absolute residual of 0.99 m indoors against 3.70 m outdoors.
  • WiFi FTM and BLE ranges carry positive biases of several metres that change with the zone. For WiFi the bias grows from 1.17 m in the transition zone to 5.94 m outdoors.
  • Satellite visibility drops sharply along the driveway row inside the hall, so satellite and terrestrial ranging degrade together at the building boundary.

IEEE/ION PLANS 202528 April – 1 May 2025, Salt Lake City

An empirical assessment of indoor-outdoor localization based on signals of opportunity from multiple systems

Albrecht Michler, Paul Schwarzbach, Jonas Ninnemann, Muhammad Ammad, Oliver Michler

Question. Can an opportunistic fusion of GNSS and terrestrial ranging keep a position solution on a path that leads from outside the hall through the building and out again?

The 12 driveway points, visited in order from T01 through row A01 to T06. The paper classifies them as outdoor, transition and indoor.

  • A grid filter fuses GNSS, UWB, WiFi FTM and BLE measurements with separate measurement models for indoor and outdoor positions.
  • Positioning outside the hall is less accurate than in the indoor and transition zones. The paper traces this to the deliberately basic GNSS processing, which used only upper L-band code measurements without NLOS mitigation.
  • Indoor and transition accuracy follows from the accuracy and availability of the UWB ranges.
